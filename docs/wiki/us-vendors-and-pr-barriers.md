
# US Waitlist Vendors and the Puerto Rico Barriers

**Summary**: Why the US products that already refill cancelled appointments (Luma Health, Phreesia, Relatient, NexHealth, Weave, Clearwave, Solutionreach) are not used by Puerto Rico medical offices, with evidence rather than opinion. None of the seven lists a Puerto Rico EHR on its integrations page; the Puerto Rico EHRs expose no scheduling API; 73.7% of Puerto Rico physician-office establishments have fewer than five employees against 54.4% in the United States, while the medical-first vendors sell to health systems; many offices still run turns rather than timed slots; and SMS to Puerto Rico carriers runs through a path that only registered Liberty in April 2026 and has no entry for Claro. Seven barrier hypotheses are given a verdict each.
**Sources**: vendor integration, pricing and legal pages; SEC filings (Phreesia FY2026 10-K, Weave 2025 10-K); Census County Business Patterns 2023; Twilio, Bandwidth and The Campaign Registry pages; Puerto Rico Senate and press; all opened 2026-10-08. Labels: **read on the page**, **snippet only**, **inferred**, **not found**.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-09

## 2. Seven barrier hypotheses, with verdicts

### H1. Integration dependency: the US tools need the EHR's integration path, and the Puerto Rico EHRs have no public scheduling interface
**Supported on the Puerto Rico side; partially on the vendor side.**
- Infomedika's Evolution EHR is certified **without any API criterion** ((g)(7), (g)(9), (g)(10) absent), runs on on-premise SQL Server, and "Interfaces fee for optional modules and/or integrations may apply" (certification disclosure, read on the page).
- Sabiamed's ClinNext 10 meets (g)(10) through Firely Server, with third-party licence fees "based on number of beds for hospitals or number of facilities for ambulatory clinics"; **no API documentation, developer portal or base URL exists on sabiamed.com** (read on the page).
- Assertus (EHRez, Medicus, ProClaim) publishes nothing reachable (site renders "Loading..."); the 2019 certification release mentions no API (read on the page).
- NeoDeck's NeoMed has a **public SMART on FHIR R4 API**, documented, OAuth2, but **GET only**, clinical resources only, **no Appointment, Schedule or Slot**, and "Only the Vendor admins of the EHR have access to add the client app" (read on the page).
- Sharper statement (inferred): the only public integration surface any Puerto Rico EHR offers is the federally required patient-access API, which is read-only clinical data by design; a refill tool needs to read the schedule and write an appointment, which no Puerto Rico EHR exposes publicly. The US vendors' "contact us / HL7 / FHIR / CSV" paths all presuppose the EHR exposing such an interface. Detail per system: [[pr-office-systems]].
- Still unknown: whether any US vendor has ever connected a Puerto Rico-domestic EHR; physician-office EHR market share 2024 to 2026 (not found).

### H2. Price relative to Puerto Rico reimbursement
**Partially supported.** The reimbursement facts are solid; the link from price to non-adoption is an inference.
- Medicare Advantage benchmarks in Puerto Rico 38% below the states after the ACA ($540.58 vs $869.31 per member per month), bids 43% lower, payments about 20% lower; traditional Medicare prices for 12 common codes about 16% lower (Roberts and Song, JAMA Health Forum 2022, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9482057 , read on the page). 2023 base rates $644 vs $1,090, 41% below (MMAPA via News is my Business, read on the page). Physician pay about $162K in Puerto Rico vs more than $229K in the United States (STAT 2024, read on the page).
- Plan Vital: "un pago mínimo de $18 PMPM al médico primario", specialists at 70 to 80% of the Medicare schedule, effective 2023-01-01 (Metro 2022-09-01 and 2022-10-20, read on the page); whether $18 is still the 2026 rate: not found.
- Vendor pricing: Weave $199 a month (vendor); the others $199 to 350 a month entry per third parties.
- Arithmetic (inferred): a $199 to 350 subscription equals the monthly capitation of 11 to 19 Vital members; on a 1,000-member panel ($18,000 a month) a $250 tool is about 1.4%. The sources show Puerto Rico pays less; they do not show the absolute price is prohibitive. The office-size argument in H4 is the stronger economic point.

### H3. SMS compliance and delivery in Puerto Rico
**Partially supported, as delivery and cost friction, not a legal bar.**
- 10DLC registration applies: Twilio's Puerto Rico guidelines say domestic long codes "Requires registration" and that for US operators with local service "you would need to follow the US Guidelines, including 10DLC Registration"; **concatenated messages "No"**; number portability "No"; toll-free "Supported", "Requires Verification" (https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/pr/sms , read on the page). AWS: 10DLC usable for Puerto Rico (read on the page).
- The Campaign Registry's carrier page names only AT&T, T-Mobile, UScellular and Verizon; **Bandwidth: "The Campaign Registry (TCR) is officially rolling out Liberty Mobile PR (Puerto Rico) as a newly supported carrier" effective 2026-04-30** (https://www.bandwidth.com/support/en/articles/14825918-tcr-adding-support-for-liberty-mobile-pr-on-april-30-2026 , read on the page). **Claro PR in TCR: not found.**
- Surcharges (Bandwidth table, read on the page): Claro Puerto Rico SMS $0.0100 per message (from 2025-11-01) against mainland registered rates of $0.0035 to 0.0050, that is two to three times (inferred); Twilio Puerto Rico outbound $0.054 per segment.
- Nine Claro and Liberty delivery incidents on Twilio's status page between September 2022 and February 2026 (read on the page; count inferred from the feed).
- Law: TCPA reaches Puerto Rico through the federal definitions of "State" (inferred from 47 U.S.C. §153). A Puerto Rico anti-scam bill (P. de la C. 632) passed the House in June 2026 and would require registered senders and informational content only; appointment reminders are not addressed in the coverage; now in the Senate (Metro 2026-06-28, read on the page).
- Design consequence (inferred): a Puerto Rico offer must fit one 160-character segment in Spanish, expect per-carrier surcharges and periodic Claro or Liberty delays, and should wait for a delivery receipt before a slot is given away.

### H4. Office size against the vendors' target segment
**Supported for the numbers; partially as a barrier (it rules out the medical-first vendors, not the dental-first SMB vendors).**
- Census County Business Patterns 2023, Puerto Rico, offices of physicians (NAICS 6211), read from the file: **3,724 establishments**; fewer than 5 employees **2,745**; 5 to 9: 735; 10 to 19: 175; 20 to 49: 54; 50 to 99: 6; 100 to 249: 3; 250 to 499: 4. 17,613 employees; annual payroll $640.8M. Sole proprietorships 1,882 (50.5%). Derived: **73.7% under 5 employees**, 93.4% under 10, 1.8% at 20 or more; in the under-5 class, 2.0 employees and $68.3K payroll per establishment (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23pr_ia_st.zip).
- United States, same file family: 218,066 establishments, **54.4%** under 5 employees, 7.5% sole proprietorships. The all-sector under-5 share is 55.3% in Puerto Rico and 55.5% in the United States, so Puerto Rico physician offices are small relative to Puerto Rico's own economy, not only because island firms are small (inferred).
- Vendor segments (read on the page): Weave's 10-K says "SMB healthcare practices... substantially all of our customers", verticals dental, optometry, veterinary, specialty medical; Phreesia's revenue per client is $106,467; Luma, Relatient and Clearwave position for health systems and specialty groups; NexHealth is dental-heavy; Solutionreach serves "practices and enterprise groups".
- Inferred: the vendors that do serve two-person offices are dental and optometry first with no Puerto Rico presence; the medical-first vendors' economics assume a client an order of magnitude larger than the median Puerto Rico office.

### H5. Decision-maker: do medical groups (IPAs, GMPs, MSOs) choose the office software?
**Partially supported.** The structure is documented; a software mandate is not found.
- Plan Vital: every enrollee "must select a PMG and a PCP"; the Grupo Médico Primario is a preferred provider network of PCPs, specialists, labs and hospitals that must open Monday to Saturday 8am to 6pm plus extended hours, and can take stop-loss risk (First Medical Provider Guidelines 2025, https://stfmvitalwebprod007.blob.core.windows.net/blobappfmvital1987a91f17/2025/01/GUIA-DEL-PROVEEDOR-FM-2025.pdf , read on the page).
- Medicare Advantage: MSO of Puerto Rico serves MMM with **21 IPAs and 37 PMGs** listed, offering network management, contracting, care management, analytics and an "Office Advantage Rewards" programme for 2,963 medical offices (https://www.mso-pr.com/quienes-somos/clientes/?lang=en , read on the page); MMM's network includes wholly owned IPAs Castellana and PHM (read on the page).
- Systems actually required of an office in these documents: payer portals for eligibility and claims and MMM's InnovaMD portal. **No IPA, PMG or MSO document or job posting that supplies or mandates an EHR or scheduling tool: not found.**
- Inferred: the sales motion runs plans, then MSOs and IPAs, then PMGs, then offices, so a plan- or IPA-level buyer exists; the claim that a solo doctor "cannot adopt a tool" is not evidenced.

### H6. "Orden de llegada" and batch scheduling
**Supported; the strongest primary evidence of the seven.**
- P. del S. 1263 (filed 2026-05-12), verbatim: "Es común que pacientes, particularmente personas de edad avanzada, acudan a sus citas desde horas de la madrugada para asegurar un turno, permaneciendo largas horas en salas de espera o incluso en las afueras de las facilidades médicas"; "La falta de controles claros sobre la programación, la programación excesiva de citas de pacientes y la ausencia de mecanismos efectivos de comunicación agravan aún más el problema"; during the pandemic "muchas oficinas médicas... comenzaron a operar mediante sistemas de citas previas y horarios escalonados... una vez superada la etapa crítica de la pandemia, en muchos casos se ha regresado a prácticas que provocan nuevamente largas esperas y concentración excesiva de pacientes". It would oblige providers to "abstenerse de citar simultáneamente un número de pacientes incompatible" with the standards and to start care within 60 minutes, and gives affected patients the option "ser colocado en una fila virtual o regresar el mismo día sin perder prioridad razonable" (https://senado.pr.gov/document_vault/medidas_legislativas/ps1263-26.pdf , read on the page). The bill received a negative committee report on 2026-08-17 and is not law.
- The sponsor, 2026-05-17: "todavía tenemos proveedores de salud que exigen al paciente tomar su turno por orden de llegada" (Metro, read on the page).
- Reglamento 7617 (2008) counts a wait over one hour as a violation; in 2013 the Procurador said the rule "nunca ha generado una multa por incumplimiento" and "sí tenemos médicos que citan a los pacientes a las 6:00 de la mañana" (Metro 2013-01-31, read on the page).
- The payer itself contemplates walk-in operation: "If a 'walk-in' system is used rather than appointment system, the Enrollee wait time shall be consistent with the severity of the clinical urgency" (First Medical Provider Guidelines 2025 §8.4.1, read on the page).
- A quantitative survey of office wait times: not found.
- Design consequence (inferred): the product's unit is "the next patient to be seen", a virtual queue that also refills cancellations, rather than a slot-level waitlist that assumes a calendar the office may not keep.

### H7. Referrals and pre-authorizations: can a visit move earlier if the referral has a date window?
**The "validity window" claim is not found; the documented rules point the other way (specialist availability is the binding constraint, not the referral).**
- Vital referral rules (First Medical Provider Guidelines 2025, read on the page): a written PCP referral is required only outside the preferred network; it "must be provided by the PCP during the same visit but no later than twenty-four (24) hours after the enrollee's request"; "If the Provider access cannot be met within the PPN within thirty (30) calendar days... the PMG will refer the enrollee to a specialist within the General Network"; providers "shall not establish specific days for the delivery of referrals". **No validity or expiry period in the 198 pages.**
- Ley 28-2024: chronic and high-cost patients "no necesitarán referidos del médico primario" (read on the page). Triple-S Advantage Platino Plus 2026: "visitar a tus especialistas sin referidos" (read on the page). Medicare Advantage referral validity: not found.
- Friction evidence: a flawed referral sends "el paciente... al médico primario para enmendarlo, lo que ocasiona demorar o perder esa cita" (Metro 2024-03-14, read on the page).

## 3. The barriers with the strongest evidence

1. **No scheduling API in the Puerto Rico EHR layer (H1).** A Puerto Rico product must run without EHR write-back (its own schedule record, or CSV and manual import), which is what the US products treat as a degraded mode.
2. **Offices that do not run timed slots (H6).** The Legislature, a sitting senator, the 2013 Procurador and the payer's provider guide all document early-arrival turns, overbooking and walk-in operation. The product's unit is the next patient to be seen.
3. **Office scale and economics (H4, with H2 as context).** Three in four offices have under five people; the medical-first vendors sell to health systems; the SMB vendors that could fit are dental-first and absent. Price per office must be low, or the buyer is the plan or the IPA.
4. **SMS delivery friction on Puerto Rico carriers (H3).** Single-segment Spanish messages, a verified toll-free fallback, and a delivery receipt before a slot is given away.
