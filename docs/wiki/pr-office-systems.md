
# Puerto Rico Office Systems and Appointments

**Summary**: What the record and scheduling systems used by Puerto Rico medical offices do about appointments, cancellations, no-shows, reschedules and waiting lists, from their own pages, help centers, certification disclosures and app-store listings. Two findings carry the design: no local system documents an automatic refill of a cancelled slot with the next waiting patient, and every public API found is read-only clinical data with no Appointment, Schedule or Slot resource. Practice Fusion is in use in Puerto Rico offices, ships Spanish reminders, and its free FHIR API cannot see or write appointments either.
**Sources**: vendor pages, ONC certification disclosures, app-store listings, job boards and press, all opened 2026-10-08; URLs inline. Labels: **read on the page**, **snippet only**, **inferred**, **not found**.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-09

---

## Why this page exists

A cancelled-slot refill needs two things from the office's system: to know a slot opened, and to book the replacement. This page records what each system used in Puerto Rico offers for that, so the product's integration story rests on what is documented rather than on what seems likely. Which systems are "used in Puerto Rico" comes from the 2024 health-information-exchange RFP (NeoMed, EHRez, Infomedika, Sabiamed; Greenway and eClinicalWorks at federally funded centers), the July 2025 E Health Partners acquisition of Assertus (EHRez 3,000 providers, Medicus about 1,000, ProClaim clearinghouse 4,500+), Inmediata's SecureEMR ("thousands of customers throughout the island", vendor claim), and Practice Fusion, which Puerto Rico offices demonstrably run (section 3).

## 2. What this means for a refill product

- **Nobody local documents refilling a cancelled slot with the next waiting patient.** The closest things are Sabiamed's daily overflow list (a hospital and clinic scheduler) and Citamed's reactivation campaign, which chases the patient who cancelled rather than the next one on a list. Citamed's and BrainHi's "lista de espera" are on-site turn queues for the waiting room (read on the page).
- **Every public API found is read-only clinical FHIR with no Appointment resource** (Practice Fusion, NeoMed, SecureEMR+; Sabiamed via Firely with no public documentation; Infomedika certified without an API criterion; no public API documentation found for Assertus). Writing to a Puerto Rico office's schedule would go through a vendor's proprietary partner track, which only Practice Fusion says it has, or outside the EHR, which Citamed openly accepts ("carga manual de archivo o reporte") (read on the page; the conclusion is inferred).
- **So the realistic entry point for a small office is the front desk confirming the cancellation**, with the queue keeping its own record, and the EHR connection as a later, vendor-by-vendor step. This is the design in the technical proposal, and it is not a shortcut: it is the only documented path.

## 3. Practice Fusion in Puerto Rico

- **What it is today.** A Veradigm product (Allscripts announced the purchase on 2018-01-08 for "$100 million in cash"). $199 per month per provider with a required annual commitment, three signing plus unlimited non-signing staff licences, two-week trial (read on the pricing page). Veradigm's September 2026 investor deck lists "Practice Fusion for small practices" as one of its two EHRs under a line that reads "Rationalize product & solution portfolio: invest, sustain, sunset/sell", without placing Practice Fusion in any category (read on the page).
- **Scheduling and reminders.** Scheduler included; "Automated text, voice, and email appointment reminders with confirmation", in English or Spanish, included; an Updox add-on adds customizable reminders and secure texting (price not listed). Documented statuses: confirmed or unconfirmed; double-booking conflict alerts; online check-in sends intake links a week and a day before (read on the page). **No-show or cancelled status, no-show reports, fees, and any waitlist: not found** (the help center could not be opened; no help article surfaced).
- **Patient Fusion.** Both the patient portal and the public directory and booking site. Booking is a request: the profile's scheduler matches the EHR schedule, requests land under Schedule, Appointment Requests, with Approve / Deny / Reschedule; profiles exist for all e-prescribing-verified providers of a practice; a practice can untick "Enable my online booking page" (read on the page).
- **API.** Developer Center with FHIR APIs, Labs, Imaging and Billing APIs, "More than 600 companies". The FHIR path is a gated registration, R4 4.0.1, US Core 6.1.0, Bulk Data, SMART 2.0; the capability statement is read and search only, **no Appointment, Schedule or Slot, no webhooks**; Practice Fusion "does not charge API fees for development, deployment or upgrade at this time". Third-party guides say practices must order an "HL7 FHIR API Add-on" and that scheduling and operational data need "separate proprietary APIs and approval" (read on the page; third-party items labelled as such).
- **Evidence of use in Puerto Rico.** The Patient Fusion directory returns 30 "General Practice" providers for each Puerto Rico location query (Puerto Rico, San Juan, Bayamón, Ponce, Manatí), all "Verified Provider", nearly all "Accepting New Patients" (read on the page). Three profiles opened (San Juan, Manatí, Caguas) all state **"This provider is not accepting online appointments currently. Call the doctor's office at (787) ... to book an appointment"**, one with a Spanish-language bio: these offices run the EHR with the public booking page switched off (sample of three, not the population). A Manatí pain clinic's nursing posting asks for "Comfortable with electronic medical records (Practice Fusion)" (read on the page). A 2014 release records partnerships with Assertus (ProClaim), Atlantic Pathology and HRP Labs, with "approximately 50 doctors" at HRP's client offices on Practice Fusion (read on the page).
- **Relevant for Puerto Rico.** Controlled-substance e-prescribing is "available in the 50 U.S. states and D.C." (read on the page), which reads as not Puerto Rico (inferred).
