
# Puerto Rico Front Desk Today

**Summary**: Public evidence of how Puerto Rico medical and dental offices handle appointments, cancellations, no-shows, reschedules and waiting lists today. The work the product automates is a named duty of $11 to $15 an hour clerks and secretaries: coordinate and confirm by phone, keep the "no show" list by hand, call patients who have no appointment coordinated, phone out cancellations and reschedules. Of 23 Puerto Rico job postings read, 17 mention appointment management and none mentions a waitlist. Booking is phone-first; no Puerto Rico office page found invites WhatsApp; no private office found publishes a cancellation window or a no-show fee. Three offices the team spoke with said the same: the secretary or office manager handles it by hand and would welcome a tool.
**Sources**: LinkedIn and job aggregators, clinic and insurer pages, Senate documents, Puerto Rico press 2013 to 2026, all opened 2026-10-08; URLs inline. Labels: **read on the page**, **snippet only**, **not found**.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-09

## 3. Channels: phone first, SMS and apps second, WhatsApp absent

- What Puerto Rico providers and plans publish (all read on the page): COSSMA takes appointment requests by SMS; MMM sends a text link for driver tracking; Triple-S offers in-app chat with TeleConsulta; First Medical, MCS and MMM apps carry directories, digital cards and pre-authorization status, no WhatsApp; the Procurador del Paciente takes complaints by phone, mail, fax and email; BrainHi, the local AI receptionist, is voice plus SMS.
- WhatsApp is also outside the health privacy law for appointment details (Meta's own terms; see `../TECHNICAL_PROPOSAL.md` §6).
- Implication for the product: the channels that match how offices already reach patients are the phone (voice) and SMS in Spanish, and the demo's simulated inbox should be labelled as standing in for those, never for WhatsApp.

## 6. What the team heard directly

Three offices, asked informally in early October 2026, no notes taken: a cardiologist (the physician answered), a dental office (the secretary answered) and a primary-care physician. All three said cancellations and reschedules are handled by the secretary or office manager by hand, and that they would welcome a tool that takes that work off that person. Three is a signal, not a sample; it is consistent with the postings above. It also covers both buyer types named in [[pr-no-show-data]] (a specialist and a dentist, paid per visit) plus the capitated primary-care case, and the primary-care doctor wanted it too: the pain the offices name is the front desk's time, not only lost revenue.

## 7. Synthesis

Appointment work in Puerto Rico is a named duty of $11 to $15 an hour clerks and secretaries: coordinating and confirming by phone, keeping the no-show list by hand, checking a no-show's next appointment in the history, calling patients who have no appointment, phoning out cancellations and reschedules, and, when a clinic cancels a day, reaching every patient one by one. No posting, office page or policy mentions a waitlist; the only place "listas de espera" appears is the Senate's 2026 definition of what an appointment system ought to contain. Booking is phone-first, with web forms at the health centers, one SMS line, insurer apps, and no WhatsApp anywhere. Supply-side pressure is documented: specialists book five months out, 800 patients waited eight to ten months, physicians admit to "poniendo pacientes extra", and the Legislature names late cancellations and overbooking as recurring practices. The gap a cancelled-slot refill fills is real and currently manual; the buyer must be reached through phone-era workflows (voice and SMS, simple UI, Spanish), and a legislated "sistema de gestión de citas" may soon make waitlists and delay notices an expectation.

**Three quotable items for a slide:** the NeoMed Center duty list ("Anotar en el listado correspondiente los pacientes 'no show'" / "Responsable de llamar a los pacientes que no tengan citas coordinadas"); the Senate's definition of an appointment system that must include "recordatorios, listas de espera... y mecanismos de notificación de atrasos"; and the pairing of "coordina las citas con cinco meses de anticipación" with "Uno termina poniendo pacientes extra y los médicos se queman".
