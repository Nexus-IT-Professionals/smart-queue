
# Puerto Rico Health Plans and Access Standards

**Summary**: What Puerto Rico's health plans and government already do about appointment access, and why a network-wide queue has a buyer. No Puerto Rico plan offers a member appointment-finding or waitlist service. ASES holds 30-day and 24-hour appointment standards for Plan Vital that it does not test: its own 2025 network report marks secret-shopper appointment checks "Not utilized", and its external quality reviewer recommended adding them. Federal wait-time standards apply from mid-2027 and mandatory independent secret shoppers from mid-2028. Only 64% of Vital members were satisfied with getting a specialist appointment. In Medicare Advantage, "Getting Appointments and Care Quickly" is a weight-2 Star measure on which Triple-S's PPO contract scores one star. BrainHi, the local incumbent, lists no payer customer.
**Sources**: ASES and docs.pr.gov documents, CMS Star Ratings files, plan websites, Procurador del Paciente statistics, press; all opened 2026-10-08; URLs inline. Labels: **read on the page**, **snippet only**, **inferred**, **not found**.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-09

## 2. ASES and Plan Vital: standards on paper, not tested

- **Contractual standards in force** (First Medical Provider Guidelines 2025, §8.4, https://stfmvitalwebprod007.blob.core.windows.net/blobappfmvital1987a91f17/2025/01/GUIA-DEL-PROVEEDOR-FM-2025.pdf , read on the page): routine primary-care evaluations "within thirty (30) calendar days"; "Specialist Services shall be provided within thirty (30) calendar days"; behavioral health 14 days; lab and imaging 14 days; urgent primary, dental and behavioral care "within twenty-four (24) hours"; dental 60 days. Beneficiary manuals carry no timeframes. The insurer contracts were renewed in July 2025 with Oversight Board approval (Metro 2025-07-24, read on the page).
- **What ASES monitors** (Network Adequacy and Access Assurances Report CY2025, submitted 2026-10-07, https://docs.pr.gov/files/ASES/Monitoreo%20%26%20Auditoria/Monitoreo/Network%20Adequacy%20and%20Access%20Assurances%20Report%20(NAAAR)/NAAAR%20Plan%20Vital%20CY2025-%20enmendado%2007-oct-2026.pdf , read on the page): geomapping quarterly and provider-directory review monthly are "Utilized"; **"Secret Shopper: Network Participation: Not utilized"; "Secret Shopper: Appointment Availability: Not utilized"**; encounter-data analysis "Not utilized"; "Review of Grievances Related to Access: Utilized, Quarterly"; for each of MMM Multihealth, Menonita, First Medical and Triple-S: "Yes, the plan complies on all standards". The 48 standards are ratios, counts and time-and-distance; none is an appointment wait-time standard.
- **The external quality reviewer** (Mercer, 2024-02-27, https://docs.pr.gov/files/ASES/Monitoreo%20%26%20Auditoria/Auditor%C3%ADa/Entidad%20de%20Revisi%C3%B3n%20de%20Calidad%20Externa%20(External%20Quality%20Review%20Organization,%20EQRO)/Informe%20Tecnico%20Anual%20de%20revision%20Externa%20de%20Calidad%20Febrero%202024.pdf , read on the page): insurers must "audit 25% of their contracted providers... to review appointment availability and timeliness"; "It was found none of the MCOs provided details of the oversight and management or mitigation of their findings"; **"It is recommended for Puerto Rico to add secret shopper surveys as oversight and monitoring to ensure providers are meeting appointment standards."** The previous review before this one was May 2019 on 2016 to 2017 data (Metro 2022-09-12, read on the page).
- **Member-reported access** (ASES Quality Strategy Evaluation, October 2024, read on the page): "Satisfaction with Getting Appointments 74.31%", **"Satisfaction with Getting Specialist Appointments 64.32%"**, "Ease of Access 72.95%"; CY2022 grievances in "Access to Care/Services from Plan or Provider": First Medical 481, MMM 995, Menonita 190, Triple-S 709. The 2024 Quality Strategy sets CAHPS "Specialists Appointment (Adult)" baselines of 74 to 79% by insurer with a +5% target (read on the page).
- **Federal rule:** 42 CFR 438.68(e) wait-time standards (routine primary care and OB-GYN within 15 business days, behavioral health 10 business days) apply to rating periods starting on or after **2027-07-09**; (f) annual independent secret-shopper surveys from **2028-07-09** (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/438.68 , read on the page). ASES's implementation plan beyond one sentence ("will be evaluating Network Adequacy Standards to align with the Medicaid Managed Care Final Rule"): not found.
- **Sanctions:** none found for appointment access. Fines on record concern credentialing (2019 to 2022, about $2.3M), non-payment of the new minimum rates including the $18 per member per month (announced 2023, later suspended), and a 2024 intent-to-fine letter to First Medical over non-payment "lo cual puede afectar el acceso" (Metro and Primera Hora, read on the page).

## 3. Medicare Advantage Star Ratings

- Measure C23 "Getting Appointments and Care Quickly" (CAHPS: care right away as soon as needed; routine appointment as soon as needed) carries **weight 2 in the 2026 and 2027 Star Ratings**, down from 4 ("The weight of patient experience/complaints and access measures decreased from four to two", CMS 2026 fact sheet, https://www.cms.gov/files/document/2026-star-ratings-fact-sheet.pdf ; measure lists https://cms.gov/files/document/2026-star-ratings-measures.pdf , https://www.cms.gov/files/document/2027-star-ratings-measures.pdf , read on the page).
- **Puerto Rico contracts, 2026 Star Ratings, C23 score and stars** (CMS data tables, read from the file, https://www.cms.gov/files/zip/2026-star-ratings-data-tables.zip ; survey fielded March to May 2025): MMM H4003 **86 (4 stars)**; MMM H4004 **85 (4 stars)**; MCS Classicare H5577 **84 (4 stars)**; Triple-S Advantage HMO H5774 **83 (3 stars)**; **Triple-S Advantage PPO H4005 79 (1 star)**. C22 "Getting Needed Care": 81, 82, 80, 81, 79. Cut points for C23: 1 star under 80, 2 stars 80 to under 82, 3 stars 82 to under 84, 4 stars 84 to under 86, 5 stars 86 and above (the 86 shown with 4 stars for H4003 reads as rounding or CMS's additional CAHPS criteria; not verified).
- Overall 2026: MMM (H4003, H4004) and MCS (H5577) hold 5 stars; Triple-S 4.5 on its main HMO contract, 3.5 to 4.5 across contracts; Triple-S's CEO on "leveraging new technologies to enhance access" (News is my Business and El Nuevo Día, read on the page). Public MMM or MCS statements about programmes for the access measure: not found.
- Context from the planning folder's evidence: 98% of Puerto Rico Medicare Advantage plans qualified for the maximum quality bonus by 2019, about $41 per member per month (JAMA Health Forum 2022); the Star link to a scheduling product is an inference, and C23 is a patient-survey measure, not a measured wait.

## 4. The Procurador del Paciente

- Published statistics stop at FY2022: complaints by classification (n=2,293): "Pobre calidad en servicio" 1,914; "Faltas de respeto y trato desigual" 108; by respondent: insurers 888, specialists 348, ancillary services 297, hospitals 278, primary physicians 252, medical groups 132. **There is no category for appointment waits or provider cancellations**; they land in "poor quality of service" (https://www.opp.pr.gov/estadisticas , read on the page).
- The Procuradora, 2024-03-14: the office receives calls about poor appointment availability but cannot count them because they are classified as "pobre calidad de servicios de salud": 1,880 of 2,302 cases in 2023; the office mediates by calling the insurer "para buscar un proveedor o ubicar al paciente en lista de espera" (Metro, read on the page).
- 8,589 complaints from 2021 to August 2024, with 28 of 120 positions filled (transition report, read on the page). A Senate resolution of 2025-10-02 orders an investigation of the office's complaint handling (read on the page).
- Ley 194-2000: care "sin demoras irrazonables"; complaints may cover "tiempos de espera, horario de operaciones"; fines $500 to $5,000; no appointment-scheduling rule. P. del S. 1263 would add complaint ground 14: "Incumplimiento con tiempos de espera, programación de citas, cancelaciones atribuibles al proveedor, ausencia de notificación de atrasos o negativa a reprogramar sin penalidad" (read on the page).

## 5. Why a network-wide queue has a buyer (inferred from the above)

- ASES holds 30-day and 24-hour standards it does not test; its reviewer told it to add secret shoppers; federal wait-time standards and mandatory independent secret shoppers arrive for rating periods from mid-2027 and mid-2028.
- Only 64% of Vital members were satisfied with getting a specialist appointment; access grievances run in the hundreds per insurer per year.
- No Puerto Rico plan offers a member appointment-finding or waitlist service; the local incumbent sells call answering and scheduling to hospitals and clinics and has no payer customer.
- On the Medicare Advantage side, access is still a weight-2 Star measure on which Triple-S's PPO contract scores one star and its HMO three, and Triple-S has named timely access as a weak spot.
- The queue across a plan's network is therefore not a speculative "radical version": it answers a standard the regulator is about to be forced to measure, with a dated clock. For the hackathon it is presented as planned, which the rules allow, with the single-office demo as the working part.
